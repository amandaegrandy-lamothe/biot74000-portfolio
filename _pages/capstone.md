## My Capstone Project

My capstone project explored how bacterial communities in soil change across very different environments and why those differences matter for plant growth. I compared flooded agricultural soil, non‑flooded agricultural soil, and subarctic Yukon soil to see how each biome’s unique microbial community supports plants under stress. The project addressed the broader question of how environmental conditions shape the microbiome and, in turn, influence plant resilience.

## The Bioinformatics Component

I used R to analyze my soil‑microbe dataset and create a stacked bar chart showing the distribution of bacterial phyla across each soil sample. R was the right choice because it allowed me to organize the data, calculate phylum‑level counts, and produce a clear visualization that highlighted differences between the flooded, non‑flooded, and subarctic biomes. This method gave me a straightforward way to compare microbial communities and identify which phyla were shared or unique across environments.

The stacked bar chart I produced showed how the major bacterial phyla were distributed across each soil sample. Flooded agricultural soil contained the highest number of isolates, with a large portion belonging to Actinomycetota, while the Yukon soil had the fewest isolates and a different phylum balance. The non‑flooded and leaf‑litter soils showed intermediate diversity, each with their own mix of Bacillota, Pseudomonadota, and Actinomycetota. Overall, the analysis highlighted which bacterial groups were most common in each biome and revealed clear differences in microbial community structure across the soil environments.


## Why It Strengthened My Capstone


Adding this bioinformatics work made my capstone stronger by providing clear, data‑driven support for the patterns I observed across the soil samples. It also made the project more credible and complete by pairing wet‑lab results with reproducible computational analysis, giving a fuller picture of how microbial communities differed between environments.
